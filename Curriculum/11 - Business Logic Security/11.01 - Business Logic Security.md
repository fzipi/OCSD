# Module 11 – Business Logic Security

This outline defines the business logic security knowledge assessed in Module 11.

## 11.1 Business logic flaws

- The meaning of business logic as the application-specific rules and workflows that define permitted behavior
- The difference between a business logic flaw and an implementation defect such as injection
- Why flaws arise from missing rules, assumptions about user behavior, and untested combinations of valid actions
- Why generic scanners find these flaws only with difficulty and why finding them needs knowledge of the application
- Financial, reputational, and compliance consequences of business logic abuse
- Misuse and abuse cases as the technique for finding flaws that normal use cases do not reveal

## 11.2 Workflow and state integrity

- Workflow bypass: skipping, repeating, or reordering steps such as payment, approval, or identity verification
- Why the server must track the current state of a workflow and must not rely on client-supplied step or status values
- Valid state transitions and the rejection of transitions that the rules do not allow
- Workflow state bound to the user's session or account, and the risk of state referenced by predictable identifiers
- Integrity of multi-step processes: abandoned flows, partial completion, expiry, and rollback
- The relationship between workflow bypass and missing authorization checks (see Module 3)

## 11.3 Race conditions and TOCTOU

- Race conditions as flaws caused by concurrent requests that interact with shared state
- Time-of-check to time-of-use (TOCTOU) as a gap between verifying a condition and acting on it
- Examples in balances, inventory, one-time coupons, and single-use tokens
- Atomic operations, transactions, locking, and optimistic concurrency as controls that close the gap
- The need to enforce uniqueness and limits in the data store as well as in application code
- Idempotency keys as a way to make repeated requests safe

## 11.4 Business rule validation and value manipulation

- Server-side calculation and validation of prices, totals, quantities, and discounts
- Client-supplied values that attackers can change: prices, quantities, currencies, and hidden fields
- Boundary conditions: negative values, zero, very large values, integer overflow, and rounding
- Cross-field and time-based rules, such as dates, deadlines, and allowed combinations
- Abuse of coupons, discounts, referrals, and loyalty points: guessable codes, reuse, and stacking
- Inventory and reservation abuse
- Authorization of high-value transactions and the integrity of transaction data between steps
- Input validation as a separate control from business rule validation (see Module 4)

## 11.5 Anti-automation and abuse limits

- Automated abuse of legitimate functions: scraping, bulk account creation, scalping, and credential stuffing
- Per-account, per-address, and per-function limits on how often a business function can be used
- The difference between rate limiting and business-flow limits such as one coupon per customer
- CAPTCHA, behavioral signals, and device signals, and their limits and costs
- Why a single control, such as an address-based limit, can be bypassed
- Monitoring for abuse patterns and its relationship to logging (see Module 7)
- Rate limiting for APIs (see Module 13) and credential stuffing defenses (see Module 2)

## 11.6 Failing securely in business processes

- The meaning of failing securely (see Module 1) applied to business rules and workflows
- Denial of the action when a rule check, authorization check, or dependency fails
- Consistent state after a failure: no partial orders, charges, or grants
- Handling of failures in upstream and downstream services, including timeouts and retries
- Error responses that do not reveal business rules to an attacker (see Module 6)

## 11.7 Testing business logic

- Abuse-case tests derived from the intended rules of a feature
- Boundary-value tests for limits, quantities, and amounts
- Workflow tests that skip, repeat, and reorder steps
- Concurrency tests that send simultaneous requests
- Automated regression tests that keep fixed flaws from returning
- Manual review and testing for flaws that automated tools do not find
