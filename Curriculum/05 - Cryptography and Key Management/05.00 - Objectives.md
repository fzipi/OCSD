# Objectives for Module 5 – Cryptography and Key Management

This module tests the developer's knowledge of how cryptography protects data in web applications and APIs, and of the mistakes that make it ineffective.

A successful candidate can:

- Distinguish confidentiality, integrity, authenticity, and non-repudiation, and identify which cryptographic mechanism supports each
- Distinguish encoding, encryption, and hashing
- Distinguish symmetric and asymmetric cryptography and recognize their typical uses
- Recognize why authenticated encryption is required and identify the role of nonces and initialization vectors (IVs)
- Distinguish hash functions, message authentication codes (MACs), and digital signatures
- Identify the role of X.509 certificates and certificate authorities in establishing trust
- Recognize why passwords require dedicated password-hashing algorithms and salts
- Recognize why security-relevant random values require a cryptographically secure random number generator
- Identify weak, deprecated, and misused algorithms and modes
- Identify common attacks against cryptographic solutions and the weaknesses they exploit
- Identify the stages of the key lifecycle: generation, storage, distribution, use, rotation, and destruction
- Recognize secure and insecure ways to store and handle keys and other secrets

## Expected competency

Candidates can recognize which cryptographic mechanism fits a stated security need.
They can identify insecure choices, such as unsalted fast hashes for passwords, reused nonces, or hardcoded keys.
They can identify the key management practices that keep cryptography effective.
